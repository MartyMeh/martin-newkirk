# Everlab — Senior Product Manager, Access

---

Dear Everlab team,

Access covers everything between a member deciding to look into something and that member getting a reviewed answer. Most of that path runs through labs, imaging providers and referrers who don't work for you. I spent eight years on a version of that problem at Car Next Door and Uber Carshare, and I'd like to bring that experience to Everlab.

At Car Next Door a trip was the product, and almost nobody who made it happen was on our payroll. An owner had to list a car and keep it available, a borrower had to turn up, installers had to fit hardware, and the key had to be where the car was. Every car needed an installation before it could join, and that capped our growth. As product manager, I led Key Swap, which let owners join without an installation and exchange the key with the borrower themselves. It made each trip harder, and the first months showed it: utilisation fell and support calls rose. We kept the model and fixed the operation around it. We removed owners whose cars didn't earn, sent self-install kits instead of technicians, and gave good owners a path to the full installation. By the time Uber acquired the business, 60% of its 10,000 cars were on Key Swap.

Much of the role you describe is operational and external, and that has been most of my work since. At Uber Carshare I built the complaints process the business needed to meet AFCA's requirements. That meant a way to complain at every transaction, escalation through to the ombudsman, reporting, and training for the frontline team. Part of it was software and part of it was people and process. That's the same call this role makes about every slow step in the chain.

Today I run product at Photonik, which I co-founded. It's solar design software, with more than 2,100 installer businesses signed up and over 50,000 designs by homeowners. I rebuilt the platform with AI-assisted development in six months, and we ship weekly. We're now trialling a marketplace in Iloilo and Cebu, in the Philippines, that matches homeowners who want solar with local installers. The homeowner journey is automated end to end, from a model of what solar and batteries would save them to a quote request. I've reworked signup several times to balance what we need to know against drop-off, and homeowner conversion has risen from 2% to 10%. The harder part looks a lot like Access. More than 30 homeowners have asked for a quote, but not yet in the areas our three approved installers cover, so building the partner network is the work now.

I haven't worked in pathology or imaging, and I'd learn the clinical chain from the people who run it. But time from decision to answer, and the share of ordered tests that complete, are the kind of numbers I've worked to before: a whole journey, measured every week, with most of it in other people's hands. I'm based in Sydney and happy to be in Surry Hills three days a week.
